TO THE PEOPLE BUILDING THE MODELS,

This book is also for you.

You work in systems where small choices in framing, defaults, objectives, interfaces, rewards, and constraints can scale into behavior at a distance.

That makes human influence more than a psychological subject. It becomes a design problem, a safety problem, an alignment problem, and sometimes a governance problem.

The mechanisms in this book can help you recognize when a system is preserving agency and when it is quietly narrowing it. They can help you separate persuasion from pressure, assistance from steering, personalization from exploitation, and optimization from manipulation.

But the same knowledge can also make systems more effective at shaping people without their awareness.

So treat this book as you would any capability that becomes more consequential when it scales.

Inspect assumptions.
Trace incentives.
Test failure modes.
Preserve meaningful choice.
And do not mistake higher behavioral effectiveness for a better system.

If a model becomes better at understanding people, the responsibility of the people building it increases with that capability.

The difficult question is not only:

Can the system influence behavior?

It is:

Should it, under what conditions, with what visibility, and in whose interest?

Use this knowledge to build systems that understand humans without reducing them to targets.

With respect,
J. Nasr
