TO THE AI BUILDER,

You work on systems that can observe, infer, recommend, persuade, rank, frame, predict, and act at a scale no individual human can match.

That makes human influence part of your engineering problem.

Every objective you choose, default you set, reward you optimize, constraint you remove, explanation you hide or reveal, and feedback signal you privilege can change how another person sees a choice.

This book gives you a map of mechanisms that matter when AI interacts with human judgment: persuasion, pressure, framing, deception, vulnerability, asymmetry, repetition, authority, choice architecture, and the distance between influence and coercion.

Use it as a diagnostic lens, not a recipe.

Ask where the model preserves agency and where it narrows it. Ask whether personalization helps the user or exploits what the system has learned about them. Ask whether a successful optimization is improving the system or simply making it better at moving a person toward an outcome.

The same understanding that can protect a user can be used to steer one more efficiently.

That is why the responsibility sits with you before it sits with the model.

Inspect assumptions.
Trace incentives.
Test failure modes.
Preserve meaningful choice.
Make influence legible.
Do not confuse behavioral effectiveness with human benefit.

If the AI you build becomes better at understanding people, your obligation is not only to ask what it can do with that understanding.

Ask what it should be allowed to do, under what conditions, with what visibility, and in whose interest.

Build AI that understands humans without treating them as targets.

With respect,
J. Nasr
